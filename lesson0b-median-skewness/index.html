KEY POINT 2.5 · MEDIAN POSITION

Where does the median sit?

The median splits an ordered set of data into two equal halves — a bottom half and a top half.

Ungrouped (list / stem-leaf / ordered freq table)

Median is at the $\left(\dfrac{n+1}{2}\right)$th value.

Even $n$: average the two middle values.

Grouped (CF graph)

Estimate the median at the $\left(\dfrac{n}{2}\right)$th value.  (Key Point 2.6)

We use $n/2$ because the graph is already an estimate.

Don’t confuse position with value. $\dfrac{n+1}{2}$ or $\dfrac{n}{2}$ tells you which data value — not the value itself.
EXAMPLE · UNGROUPED MEDIAN

Back-to-back stem-and-leaf — DIY store

Customers per hour.

Monday (12) | | Saturday (15) 8 6 3 1 0 0 | 2 | 2 3 4 6      4 3 1 1 0 | 3 | 5 5 6 8 9 9               1 | 4 | 0 1 3 7 9

Key. $0\mid2\mid2$ = 20 customers Mon, 22 customers Sat.

Saturday · $n=15$

Position $=\dfrac{15+1}{2}=8$th value.

Read L→R: row 1 has 4, row 2 has 6 more ⇒ 8th value is 2nd in row 2 $=\mathbf{38}$.

Monday · $n=12$

Position $=\dfrac{12+1}{2}=6.5$th — average the 6th and 7th.

Read R→L: 6th is end of row 1 $=28$; 7th is start of row 2 $=30$.

Median $=\dfrac{28+30}{2}=\mathbf{29}$.

Back-to-back trap. The left side ascends right-to-left. Count in the direction the leaves grow.

KEY POINT 2.6 · GROUPED DATA

Estimating the median from a CF graph

With grouped / continuous data we cannot see individual values. We estimate the median by reading from a cumulative frequency graph.

The recipe
  1. Find $\dfrac{n}{2}$ on the vertical (CF) axis.
  2. Draw a horizontal line to the curve.
  3. Drop a perpendicular to the horizontal axis.
  4. Read off the data value — that is your estimated median.

read across · drop down

Position vs value.  $n/2$ is where on the CF axis you start; the median is where on the data axis you finish.

APPLIED · MUSEUM ARTEFACTS

300 artefacts — estimate the median mass

The CF graph has total $n=300$, so we estimate the median at $\dfrac{n}{2}=\mathbf{150}$.

§2.4 · CHOOSING AN AVERAGE

Which of the three should we use?

A student scores $3,\ 4,\ 6,\ 7,\ 8,\ 11,\ 12,\ 13,\ 17,\ 17$ out of 20 on ten tests.

Mode

17

Highest — impresses parents.

Mean

9.8

All values count.

Median

9.5

Middle $=\dfrac{8+11}{2}$.

Both mean and median say < 10/20. The mode gives an answer, but a misleading one. The “right” average depends on why you’re reporting it.
THE COMPARISON TABLE

Advantages and disadvantages

AdvantagesDisadvantages
Mode Unaffected by extremes. Useful for popular sizes / qualitative data. Works for any data type. Ignores most values. Rarely used in further calculations.
Mean Uses every value. Standard for further calculations. Most widely understood. Needs every value. Pulled by extreme values.
Median Found without knowing every value. Relatively unaffected by extremes. Uses only the order of values — ignores magnitudes.

Rule of thumb

Symmetric, no extremes → mean.  Skewed or outliers → median.  Popular category → mode.

Mean breaks — median holds

Dataset: $\ 40,\ 40,\ 70,\ 100,\ 130,\ 250$.

Mode and median are unchanged. The mean jumps by $100\%$.

§2.5 · SKEWNESS

What the shape tells you

Symmetric

Positive skew · long right tail

Negative skew · long left tail

Positive skew

mode $<$ median $<$ mean

Tail of large values pulls the mean up.

Negative skew

mean $<$ median $<$ mode

Tail of small values pulls the mean down.

EXAM TWISTS

Where students lose marks

Twist 1Position vs value — writing $\dfrac{n+1}{2}$ as the answer instead of the data value at that position.
Twist 2$n/2$ (CF graph) vs $\dfrac{n+1}{2}$ (ungrouped). Pick the right one.
Twist 3Back-to-back stem-leaf — one side is read right-to-left.
Twist 4“Which average and why” — one mark for the choice, one mark for the reason.
Twist 5Deducing skew from mean vs median — which is bigger tells you the direction.
Twist 6Effect of changing one value — mean shifts; median & mode usually don’t.

One mantra for shape. mean $>$ median ⇒ positive skew.   mean $<$ median ⇒ negative skew.

Classwork Q1 · Ex 2E Q1 (a, b) · p. 44

A dentist’s patients over 20 days: stems 0, 1, 2 with leaves $\ 0\!\mid\!4\,4\,4\,5\,6\,6\,6\,7\ ;\ 1\!\mid\!4\,5\,5\,6\,6\,7\,7\,8\,8\,9\ ;\ 2\!\mid\!0\,1$.   Key: $1\!\mid\!5=15$ patients.

(a) Find the median.    (b) On 8 of these 20 days the dentist was late. To justify being late, would they quote the median or the mean? Explain.

Answer(a) $n=20$, position $=\dfrac{21}{2}=10.5$th. 10th and 11th values are both $15$. Median $=\mathbf{15}$.
(b) $\Sigma x=42+165+41=248$, mean $=\dfrac{248}{20}=\mathbf{12.4}$. Median $(15)>$ mean $(12.4)$, so the dentist would quote the median — it makes the “average workload” look higher.

H2 · Ex 2E Q4 (a, b) · p. 45 · CF graph, split by median

A CF graph shows the times taken by $112$ people to complete a puzzle. (a) Estimate the median time. (b) The median splits the group in two; find the median time of each half.

Method(a) $\dfrac{n}{2}=56$. Read across from $CF=56$, drop down — estimate the median (from the graph, $\approx \mathbf{5.5\ \text{min}}$).
(b) Lower half: $\dfrac{56}{2}=28$. Read across at $CF=28$ → lower-half median $\approx \mathbf{3.5\text{ to }4\ \text{min}}$.
Upper half: $56+28=84$. Read across at $CF=84$ → upper-half median $\approx \mathbf{7\ \text{min}}$.
Your exact reading from the printed graph may differ by $\pm 0.5$ min — that is acceptable.

H3 · Ex 2E Q5 (a, b) · p. 45 · CF polygon

Masses $m$ kg of $148$ objects: $cf$ at $m<0,\,0.2,\,0.3,\,0.5,\,0.7,\,0.8$ is $0,16,28,120,144,148$. Construct a CF polygon and estimate the number of objects with masses (a) within $0.1$ kg of the median, (b) more than $200$ g from the median.

AnswerMedian at $\dfrac{n}{2}=74$: lies in the $0.3\le m<0.5$ class. Interpolate: $0.3+\dfrac{74-28}{120-28}\times 0.2=0.3+0.1=\mathbf{0.4}$ kg.
(a) Within $0.1$ kg = $[0.3,\,0.5]$ kg → count $=120-28=\mathbf{92}$ objects.
(b) More than $0.2$ kg from median = $m<0.2$ or $m>0.6$. $cf(0.6)=120+\dfrac{0.1}{0.2}(144-120)=132$. Count $=16+(148-132)=\mathbf{32}$ objects.

H4 · Ex 2E Q6 · p. 45 · Effect of one wrong value

Quiz marks: $11,13,15,15,17,18,19,20$. The $11$ was a typing error — it should be $1$. What is the effect on the mode, mean and median?

AnswerBefore: mode $=15$, median $=\dfrac{15+17}{2}=16$, mean $=\dfrac{128}{8}=16$.
After ($1,13,15,15,17,18,19,20$): mode $=\mathbf{15\ \text{unchanged}}$, median $=\mathbf{16\ \text{unchanged}}$, mean $=\dfrac{118}{8}=\mathbf{14.75}$ (down by $1.25$).
Only the mean responds to the extreme change — median and mode do not.

H6 · Ex 2E Q3 (c) · p. 44 · Stretch

Using the table from Q2: some values were incorrectly recorded as $8$ instead of $4$. Given that the true median is $5.5$, how many values were incorrect?

AnswerLet $k$ be the mis-recorded count. True frequencies: $f_4=14+k,\ f_8=15-k$. Cumulative becomes $\{14+k,\,27+k,\,31+k,\,43+k,\,58\}$.
For median $=5.5$ with $n=58$ (pos $29.5$), the 29th must land on $x=5$ and the 30th on $x=6$ ⇒ $27+k\ge 29$ and $27+k<30$ ⇒ $\mathbf{k=2}$.
Check: new $cf=\{16,29,33,45,58\}$ → 29th $=5$, 30th $=6$, median $=5.5$ ✓
