PREREQUISITE · LESSON 0a · 35–45 MIN

Coded Data — The Mean

One idea that unlocks every coded-totals question for the rest of Paper 5:

code  →  compute  →  decode

Cambridge 9709 Paper 5 · Ch 2 §2.2 · Key Points 2.3 & 2.4 · Worked Examples 2.9–2.12

MOTIVATION

Why code at all?

Find the mean of  $101,\ 103,\ 104,\ 109,\ 113$.

Trick. Subtract $100$ from every value:

$1,\ 3,\ 4,\ 9,\ 13$

Mean of the coded values is $\ \dfrac{1+3+4+9+13}{5}=6$.

Add $100$ back to decode: $\ \bar{x}=6+100=\mathbf{106}$.

The golden rule

Code → shrink the arithmetic.  Compute → mean of the small numbers.  Decode → reverse what you did.

CORE · ADDITION CODING

The shift: $\ y=x-b$

Subtract $b$ from every value. Every value slides left by $b$ on the number line — so does the mean.

values of $x-b$mean of $x-b$
← translated by $-b$ ←
values of $x$mean of $x$

Algebra you’ll reuse all year:

$\sum(x-b)=\sum x-nb$

(There are $n$ copies of $b$ being subtracted — one per data value.)

KEY POINT 2.3 · ADDITION CODING

The decoding formulas

Ungrouped

$\bar{x}=\dfrac{\sum(x-b)}{n}+b$

Grouped

$\bar{x}=\dfrac{\sum(x-b)f}{\sum f}+b$

One-liner to memorise

$\bar{x}=\operatorname{mean}(x-b)+b$
Watch out. The code can be $x-b$ or $x+b$.  If the code is $x+b$, then $\bar{x}=\operatorname{mean}(x+b)-b$ — you undo whatever was done.
WORKED EXAMPLE 2.9 · COMBINED GROUPS

Two groups, two codings

$b$ = age of a boy, $g$ = age of a girl. 5 years ago, the 10 boys satisfied $\Sigma(b-5)=127.0$. In 5 years’ time the 15 girls will satisfy $\Sigma(g+5)=351.0$. Find the mean age today of

(a) the 10 boys    (b) the 15 girls    (c) all 25 combined.

aBoys’ past mean was $\dfrac{127}{10}=12.7$.  Today: $\ \bar{b}=\dfrac{127}{10}+5=\mathbf{17.7\ \text{years}}$.
bGirls’ future mean is $\dfrac{351}{15}=23.4$.  Today: $\ \bar{g}=\dfrac{351}{15}-5=\mathbf{18.4\ \text{years}}$.
cCombine the totals, not the means. $\ \Sigma b=177,\ \Sigma g=276$.
$\displaystyle \bar{\text{all}}=\frac{\Sigma b+\Sigma g}{10+15}=\frac{177+276}{25}=\mathbf{18.12\ \text{years}}$.
Trap. Combined mean is never the average of the two means. It is always $\dfrac{\Sigma x+\Sigma y}{n_x+n_y}$.
WORKED EXAMPLE 2.10 · CODED FREQUENCY TABLE

40 values of $x$ in a grouped table

$x-3$ $0\le\cdot<18$ $18\le\cdot<24$ $24\le\cdot<32$
frequency $f$ $9$ $13$ $18$

Estimate $\bar{x}$.

Step 1. Class mid-values of the coded variable: $9,\ 21,\ 28$.

Step 2. $\displaystyle \bar{x}=\frac{\Sigma(x-3)f}{\Sigma f}+3=\frac{9(9)+21(13)+28(18)}{40}+3$

$=\dfrac{81+273+504}{40}+3=\dfrac{858}{40}+3=21.45+3=\mathbf{24.45}$.

Tip. You do not need to decode the mid-values first. Code, average, then decode once at the end.

CORE · MULTIPLICATION CODING

Scaling as well as shifting: $\ y=ax-b$

Four teachers earn $\$3600,\ \$4200,\ \$3700,\ \$4500$ — mean $\$4000$.

Each gets a 10% raise, then pays $\$50$ extra tax. What is the new mean?

New salaries: $\ \$3910,\ \$4570,\ \$4020,\ \$4900\ \Rightarrow\ $ mean $=\$4350$.

Data coded as $\ ax-b\ $ has mean $\ a\bar{x}-b$.

Check: $\ 1.1\times 4000-50=4350\ $✓

The order matters. To recover $\bar{x}$ from $\overline{ax-b}$:  undo $-b$ first, then undo $\times a$.
KEY POINT 2.4 · MULTIPLICATION CODING

The full decoding formulas

Ungrouped

$\bar{x}=\dfrac{1}{a}\!\left[\dfrac{\Sigma(ax-b)}{n}+b\right]$

Grouped

$\bar{x}=\dfrac{1}{a}\!\left[\dfrac{\Sigma(ax-b)f}{\Sigma f}+b\right]$

One-liner to memorise

$\bar{x}=\dfrac{1}{a}\,\big[\operatorname{mean}(ax-b)+b\big]$

Expansion identity (crucial for Paper 5):

$\Sigma(ax-b)=a\Sigma x-nb$
WORKED EXAMPLE 2.11 · UNIT CHANGE

$\Sigma x$ in m$^{2}$ → total in cm$^{2}$

The total area of cloth from a factory is $\Sigma x$ in square metres. Find an expression in $x$ for the total area in square centimetres.

Convert the unit:

$1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}\ \Rightarrow\ 1\text{ m}^2=100^2\text{ cm}^2=10\,000\text{ cm}^2$.

So each $x$ metres$^{2}$ becomes $10\,000x$ cm$^{2}$ — total $=\ \mathbf{\Sigma 10\,000x}\ =\ \mathbf{10\,000\,\Sigma x}$.

Why it matters. Multiplying every data value by a constant $k$ multiplies the sum by $k$. This is the arithmetic behind every unit-change question (grams ↔ kg, m/s ↔ km/h, mm ↔ cm).

WORKED EXAMPLE 2.12 · UNDO IN ORDER

From $\Sigma(2x-3)=104$ (20 values) find $\bar{x}$

Method 1 — decode the mean

1Mean of coded values: $\ \overline{2x-3}=\dfrac{104}{20}=5.2$.
2Undo $-3$: $\ 2\bar{x}=5.2+3=8.2$.
3Undo $\times 2$: $\ \bar{x}=\dfrac{8.2}{2}=\mathbf{4.1}$.

Method 2 — expand the brackets

$\Sigma(2x-3)=2\Sigma x-20(3)=104\ \Rightarrow\ 2\Sigma x=164\ \Rightarrow\ \Sigma x=82\ \Rightarrow\ \bar{x}=\dfrac{82}{20}=\mathbf{4.1}$.

Either method works. Use expansion when the question also asks for $\Sigma x$.

EXAM TWISTS

The same skill, seven disguises

Twist 1Sign flip — code is $x+b$ (not $x-b$). Decode by subtracting $b$.
Twist 2Mixed constants — e.g. $\Sigma(ax-b)$ and $\Sigma(bx-a)$ in the same question.
Twist 3Solve for $n$ — given $\bar{x}$ and $\Sigma(x-b)$, find how many data values.
Twist 4Solve for $b$ — given two codings of the same data, deduce the constant.
Twist 5Combined groups — add the totals, divide by total frequency.
Twist 6Unit change — mm to cm, g to kg, m/s to km/h: multiply $\Sigma$ by the conversion factor.
Twist 7Grouped data — code the mid-values, don’t decode them.

One mantra beats all seven.  Do whatever was done — then undo it in reverse order.

Classwork Q1 · Ex 2D Q1 · p. 38

The masses, $x$ kg, of 12 objects are such that $\bar{x}=0.475$. Find the value of $\Sigma 1000x$ and state what it represents.

Answer$\Sigma x=0.475\times 12=5.7$ kg, so $\Sigma 1000x=\mathbf{5700}$. It is the total mass of the 12 objects in grams.

Classwork Q2 · Ex 2D Q4 · p. 38

Speeds, measured in metres per second, are denoted by $x$. Find the constant $k$ such that $kx$ denotes the speeds in kilometres per hour.

Answer$1\text{ m/s}=\dfrac{1}{1000}\text{ km} \div \dfrac{1}{3600}\text{ h}=\dfrac{3600}{1000}=3.6$ km/h. So $\mathbf{k=3.6}$ and $3.6x$ km/h.
HOMEWORK · PRACTICE SET (5 QUESTIONS)

All from Exercise 2D — multiplication coding

H1 · Ex 2D Q2 · p. 38 · Unit scaling, two ways

Total mass of gold from a mine is $\Sigma x$ grams. Find an expression in $x$ for the total mass in:
(a) carats, given 1 carat $=200$ mg    (b) kilograms.

Answer(a) $1$ carat $=0.2$ g, so $x$ g $=\dfrac{x}{0.2}=5x$ carats. Total $=\mathbf{5\Sigma x}$ carats.
(b) $1$ kg $=1000$ g, so $x$ g $=\dfrac{x}{1000}$ kg. Total $=\mathbf{\dfrac{1}{1000}\Sigma x}=\mathbf{0.001\,\Sigma x}$ kg.

H2 · Ex 2D Q3 · p. 38 · Area unit change

Land for growing wheat is $\Sigma w$ hectares. Find an expression in $w$ for the total area in square kilometres, given $1$ hectare $=10\,000$ m$^2$.

Answer$1$ km$^2=10^6$ m$^2$, so $1$ ha $=\dfrac{10\,000}{10^6}=0.01$ km$^2$.
Total $=0.01\,\Sigma w=\mathbf{\dfrac{\Sigma w}{100}}$ km$^2$.

H3 · Ex 2D Q5 · p. 38 · Grouped data in the wrong unit

Wind speeds $x$ mph at a coastal location on 40 consecutive days:

$15\!\le\! x\!<\!17$: 9 $17\!\le\! x\!<\!20$: 13 $20\!\le\! x\!<\!24$: 14 $24\!\le\! x\!<\!25$: 4

Abel knows $5$ miles $\approx 8$ km. (a) Explain how to estimate the mean in km/h without converting boundaries. (b) Estimate the mean wind speed in km/h.

Answer(a) Find the mean in mph using class mid-values, then multiply once by $\dfrac{8}{5}=1.6$.
(b) Mid-values: $16,\ 18.5,\ 22,\ 24.5$.
$\bar{x}_{\text{mph}}=\dfrac{9(16)+13(18.5)+14(22)+4(24.5)}{40}=\dfrac{144+240.5+308+98}{40}=\dfrac{790.5}{40}=19.7625$ mph.
$\bar{x}_{\text{km/h}}=19.7625\times 1.6=\mathbf{31.62\ \text{km/h}}$.

Algebra — Ex 2D Q6 & Q7

H4 · Ex 2D Q6 · p. 38 · Mixed constants

Given that $15$ values of $x$ satisfy $\Sigma(3x-2)=528$, find $\bar{x}$ and find the value of $b$ such that $\Sigma(0.5x-b)=138$.

Answer$\Sigma(3x-2)=3\Sigma x-15(2)=3\Sigma x-30=528\ \Rightarrow\ \Sigma x=186\ \Rightarrow\ \bar{x}=\dfrac{186}{15}=\mathbf{12.4}$.
$\Sigma(0.5x-b)=0.5(186)-15b=93-15b=138\ \Rightarrow\ -15b=45\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{b=-3}$.

H5 · Ex 2D Q7 · p. 38 · Two codings, two unknowns

For 20 values of $x$: $\Sigma(ax-b)=400$ and $\Sigma(bx-a)=545$. Given that $\bar{x}=6.25$, find $a$ and $b$.

Answer$\Sigma x=6.25\times 20=125$.
$a(125)-20b=400\ \Rightarrow\ 25a-4b=80$  (i)
$b(125)-20a=545\ \Rightarrow\ -4a+25b=109$  (ii)
$(\text{i})\times 25$: $\ 625a-100b=2000$.   $(\text{ii})\times 4$: $\ -16a+100b=436$.
Add: $609a=2436\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{a=4}$. Sub back: $25(4)-4b=80\ \Rightarrow\ \mathbf{b=5}$.
Check: $\Sigma(4x-5)=4(125)-20(5)=400$ ✓  $\Sigma(5x-4)=5(125)-20(4)=545$ ✓.
